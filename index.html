ABOUT THE OPPORTUNITY MISSION

Opportunity (MER-B) was a robotic rover active on Mars from 2004 to 2018. Launched on July 7, 2003, as part of NASA's Mars Exploration Rover program, it touched down in Meridiani Planum on January 25, 2004.

Designed for a 90-sol mission, Opportunity operated for 5,111 sols (over 14 years), traveling 45.16 kilometers—making it the longest-running off-world mission in human history.

Key discoveries included hematite concretions ("blueberries") proving ancient liquid water, the exploration of Victoria and Endeavour craters, and evidence of habitable ancient environments.

THE FAREWELL · SOL 5111
“My battery is low and it’s getting dark.”

On June 10, 2018 (Sol 5111), a catastrophic planet-encircling dust storm blotted out the Martian sky above Perseverance Valley. Opportunity transmitted its final telemetry packet, reporting atmospheric tau > 10.8 and battery reserves dropping below survival thresholds.

Over the following eight months, NASA engineers sent over a thousand recovery commands. On February 13, 2019, from NASA JPL, the mission was officially declared complete. Rest well, Oppy.
