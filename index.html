ABOUT THE OPPORTUNITY MISSION

Opportunity (MER-B) was a robotic rover active on Mars from 2004 to 2018. Launched on July 7, 2003, as part of NASA's Mars Exploration Rover program, it touched down in Meridiani Planum on January 25, 2004.

Engineered for an operational baseline of 90 Martian sols (about 92.5 Earth days), Opportunity survived for 5,352 sols (14 years, 138 days), traversing 45.16 kilometers (28.06 miles).

• LAUNCH: July 7, 2003 (Cape Canaveral SLC-17B)
• LANDING: January 25, 2004 (Eagle Crater, Meridiani)
• FINAL CONTACT: June 10, 2018 (Perseverance Valley)
• TOTAL DISTANCE: 45.16 km (Off-world record)
THE FAREWELL · SOL 5111
“My battery is low and it’s getting dark.”

On June 10, 2018, as a global dust storm enveloped Mars and blocked out the sun, Opportunity transmitted its final engineering telemetry package from Perseverance Valley on the rim of Endeavour Crater.

After over 1,000 recovery signals sent by NASA's Deep Space Network went unanswered, the mission was officially declared complete on February 13, 2019. Opportunity sleeps forever beneath the red sands, its scientific legacy immortal.
