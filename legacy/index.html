The "Hole-in-One" Airbag Touchdown

After a 483-million-kilometer interplanetary cruise, Opportunity bounced across the Martian surface wrapped in protective airbags, coming to rest precisely inside the 22-meter-wide Eagle Crater. The rover opened its petals directly in front of the first exposed sedimentary bedrock ever examined on Mars.

"We hit the interplanetary sweet spot. We rolled into a crater that acted like an open geological book." — Steve Squyres, MER Principal Investigator

Pancam Mast Assembly (PMA)

Elevates stereo Pancam and Navcam sensors 1.5 meters above the ground, replicating human eye height. Incorporates 360° azimuthal pan and ±90° elevation gimbals with 16 narrow-band multispectral filters.

OPERATING VOLTAGE: 28–32 V DC
ANGULAR RESOLUTION: 0.27 mrad/pixel
OPERATING TEMP: -40°C to +40°C
MOBILITY & CHASSIS

Rocker-Bogie 6-Wheel Drive

Passive kinematic suspension with differential crossbar. Climbs over obstacles larger than wheel diameter (25 cm) without springs or single-point rollover. 4 corner steering actuators provide 360° point turn capability.

  • MAX SPEED: 0.05 m/s (5 cm/s)
  • DRIVE MOTORS: 6x Brushless DC In-Hub
  • MAX OBSTACLE: 25 cm (10 in)
POWER & THERMAL SUBSYSTEM

GaAs Triple-Junction Solar

1.3 m² planar solar array generating ~140 W at Martian noon. 2x rechargeable Lithium-Ion batteries (8 Ah each) store energy for overnight sleep. 8 Radioisotope Heater Units (RHUs, 1 W thermal each, ²³⁸Pu) protect interior avionics.

  • ARRAY AREA: 1.3 m² (GaAs/Ge)
  • BATTERY CAPACITY: 2x 8 Ah Li-ion (32 V)
  • RHU ISOTOPE: 8x ²³⁸Pu (1 W_th each)
AVIONICS & AUTONOMY

Radiation-Hardened RAD6000

BAE Systems RAD6000 32-bit RISC CPU clocked at 20 MHz. Runs Wind River VxWorks real-time operating system (RTOS) with autonomous hazard navigation (AutoNav) stereoscopic terrain processing.

  • CPU CLOCK: 20 MHz RISC
  • MEMORY: 128 MB DRAM + 256 MB Flash
  • REAL-TIME OS: VxWorks RTOS
[SYS_LOG // 04 · FINAL TRANSMISSION & TRIBUTE]

PERSEVERANCE VALLEY · MERIDIANI PLANUM · SOL 5111

"MY BATTERY IS LOW
AND IT'S GETTING DARK."

On June 10, 2018 (Sol 5111), Opportunity beamed its last transmission to Earth. A planet-wide dust storm smothered the sun over Perseverance Valley. Atmospheric optical depth (τ) climbed above 10.8, throttling solar wattage down to 22 Wh—insufficient to power the heaters that prevented its electronics from freezing.

FINAL PING: 12:45 UTC · SOL 5111 · 1,000+ RECOVERY COMMANDS TRANSMITTED ACROSS 8 MONTHS
